INTRO - NEW SPECIES

In the world of Walter Van Beirendonck, a jacket is never just a jacket. It is a protective totem, a digital skin, and a political manifesto. While the rest of the fashion world was obsessed with the "real," Walter was busy engineering a New Species. He looked at the human form not as a finished product, but as a prototype in a constant state of evolution.

THE TECHNO-SHAMAN

[1985] The Birth of Walter V. United Trust

The Techno-Shaman collection marked Van Beirendonck's emergence as a force in fashion. Drawing inspiration from shamanic rituals and emerging technology, he created garments that blurred the line between the spiritual and the digital.

Through this collection, Van Beirendonck established himself as a visionary who saw fashion as a medium for transformation—both personal and societal.

AESTHETICTERRORISTS

2001-2002 | Rebellion Through Surrealism

The Aestheticterrorists collection represents a peak in Van Beirendonck's exploration of surrealism and political commentary. Created during a period of global uncertainty, these designs served as both armor and weapon—protecting the wearer while challenging the viewer.

This collection featured distorted silhouettes, unexpected material combinations, and imagery that defied conventional beauty standards. Each piece was a manifesto, declaring independence from mainstream fashion and embracing the power of the unconventional.

THE COURT JESTER

Rebellion Through Truth

2010 - PROTEST

In 2010, Van Beirendonck turned his focus to protest and activism, using fashion as a platform for truth-telling. This collection featured bold graphics, political slogans, and designs that directly engaged with contemporary social issues.

The garments became canvases for dissent, each piece carrying messages of resistance, hope, and the urgent need for change. This period marked a shift toward more explicitly political work, while maintaining the designer's signature aesthetic of boldness and innovation.

DAWLEETOO

The Human vs. The Machine

Dawleetoo explores the tension between humanity and technology, questioning what it means to be human in an increasingly digital world. Van Beirendonck's designs in this collection feature mechanical elements, digital patterns, and organic forms in constant dialogue.

This work reflects on the cyborg condition—the merging of flesh and machine, the blurring of boundaries between natural and artificial. Each piece asks: where does the human end and the machine begin? And perhaps more importantly, should there be a boundary at all?
